Who builds it

The project does not come from the offices of a software company that tried to guess what schools need, but straight from the teacher's desk. It was created by Laura Miron — a mathematics teacher at the “William Shakespeare” High School in Timișoara and the author of the platform Matematică cu Laura Miron — together with her husband, to take the weight of the mountains of paper off the desk.

It is a tool built by someone who lives the reality of education every day, meant to give teachers back their most precious asset: a little free time.

Why it came about

Grading a test is not hard, it is long. A test with six exercises, given to three classes, means about a hundred papers, each scored step by step. And the points must be the same for every student: the last paper is graded the same way as the first.

MQGrader takes over the repetitive part: reading the photographed papers, finding each step in the marking scheme, adding up the points. The part that needs judgement stays where it belongs: with the teacher.

What it does

  • It reads the papersthat students photograph and send from their phones, with no account needed.
  • It proposes the points step by stepby the marking scheme, with partial points for the steps solved.
  • It sets the unsure points asideand says why, for the teacher to decide.
  • It groups the photos by studentso you do not get a heap of nameless files.
  • It prepares the resultseach student's result, the class report, a PDF for each student and a CSV file for the grade book.

What it does not do

  • It does not give the final grade instead of the teacher.The proposed points do not reach the student until the teacher has seen them.
  • It does not grade by an unconfirmed marking scheme.A generated marking scheme waits until the teacher reviews and confirms it.
  • It does not ask students for an account, an app or an email address.The QR code on the test and the phone's camera are enough.
  • It publishes nothing by itself.The results reach the students only when the teacher publishes them.

Where the product is now

MQGrader is still in development and changes often. Accounts open on request: you ask for an account, confirm your email address, and an administrator approves it. With a demo account you can grade one test, with at most 35 papers.

What each kind of account includes is on the page What MQGrader costs.
