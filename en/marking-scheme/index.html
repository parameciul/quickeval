The marking scheme

The marking scheme is the list of steps by which you give the points. Without it, you grade each paper by what you remember from the one before. Below you learn what a good marking scheme holds, where it comes from and how to get one for your tests.

What a marking scheme is

The marking scheme describes the points of a test: for each exercise, the steps that earn points and how many points each is worth. It is not a solution of the test, but the rule by which the grade is given.

A good marking scheme answers three questions for each exercise: what must appear in the paper for the point to be given, how much each step is worth and what earns no points. The rest — how nicely the paper is written, how tidy it is, whether it has a frame — does not count in the points.

Where a test's marking scheme comes from

It depends on the test:

  • For national exams (in Romania, the Evaluarea Națională and the baccalaureate), the marking scheme is set by the ministry and published after the exam. You do not have it in advance, and it is applied exactly when grading.
  • For class tests, the marking scheme is yours. You write it, take it from a workbook or adapt it from the official one. Nobody checks it for you, so it must be as clear to you at the thirtieth paper as at the first.
  • For short quizzes, which you grade on the spot, the marking scheme sometimes stays only in your head. That works, until the day you have two classes to grade.

What a good marking scheme for a class test looks like

It needs no special format, but it must hold a few things:

  1. One line for every point, with the points written next to it. “Right solution — 5p” does not help you grade; “equation written correctly — 1p, discriminant worked out — 1p, the roots — 2p, the check — 1p” does.

  2. The total, written once, at the start, together with the starting points, if you use them.

  3. The other solutions, if you accept them. Otherwise, a student who solves it correctly by another method ends up with less than one who followed the marking scheme.

  4. The small mistakes: an arithmetic slip that does not change the result, a missing unit of measure, an incomplete drawing. Decide once whether you take points off for them, so you do not decide differently on each paper.

How to get a marking scheme in MQGrader

You have two ways. You choose one when you create the test, or later, from the test's page:

1You upload your marking scheme.

It is a file with the points, as you already have it. The grading uses it exactly as it is.

2You make the marking scheme from the test.

You upload only the test, and MQGrader makes the marking scheme automatically: the steps of each exercise and their points. Then you review it, adjust it if needed and confirm it.

The second way helps when the test is ready but the marking scheme is not. It has one simple rule: nothing is graded by a marking scheme you have not confirmed. You read the generated marking scheme and, if it is good, you confirm it. If it does not suit you, you adjust it or replace it with a file of your own, and the grading follows that one.

Why the marking scheme is confirmed

A generated marking scheme is a starting point, not a decision. If it were used straight away, you would have 30 papers scored by a marking scheme you had not read, and their grading would have to start over.

Frequently asked questions

  • Can I grade without a marking scheme?

    On paper you can, but then the points depend on what you remember from the paper before. In MQGrader, if you have no marking scheme ready, you make one from the test automatically, and you review it before anything is graded.

  • Who sets the final points?

    The teacher. The proposed points follow the marking scheme, and the unsure points wait for your review: you accept or change them. The decision stays yours.

  • What are the 10 starting points?

    In Romania's national exams, the paper has 100 points, 10 of which are given as starting points. For class tests you can work the same way or with any other total, as long as the marking scheme says so.

  • Can I upload a marking scheme I wrote?

    Yes. The marking scheme you upload is used exactly as it is. A generated marking scheme is only a draft, which you review, adjust or replace.

  • Is anything graded before I confirm the marking scheme?

    No. As long as the marking scheme is not confirmed, grading does not start, so that no student gets points by a marking scheme you have not approved.

  • What happens if a student solves it differently from the marking scheme?

    The exercise comes to you as a point to check, with the reason. If the solution is right, you give it the full points, even if the method is not the one in the marking scheme.
