How to grade tests by the marking scheme

Grading by the marking scheme means points you can explain: every point given has a reason, and two students with the same solution get the same points. Below you find the stages, in order, what can be automated and where the teacher has to step in.

What grading by the marking scheme means

The marking scheme splits every exercise into steps, and every step has its own points. You do not look only at the final answer and you do not give “all or nothing”: a student who started right and stopped halfway gets the points for the steps they did.

This gives three advantages:

  • The same points for the same solution, whether you grade it on Monday evening or on Friday morning.
  • Partial points you can explain to the student, because every point belongs to a step of the solution, not only to the final grade.
  • A grade that is easy to redo: if you change the points of one step, you redo only the total, not the whole grading.

In Romania's national exams (the Evaluarea Națională and the baccalaureate), the marking scheme is published after the exam and has 100 points, 10 of which are given as starting points. A class test does not have to follow this model: you only need a clear marking scheme that you apply the same way to every student.

The six stages of a test, in order

Every test goes through the same six stages, whether 12 students take it or 34. The order matters, because each stage prepares what the next one needs. The screens are from the app, on a sample class, with made-up names.

1

Prepare

You

You need two files: the test and the marking scheme. Many teachers have the test in a document, and the marking scheme only in their head or on a handwritten sheet. It is worth writing the marking scheme in an easy-to-read form, with one line for each point: with 30 papers, you will read it 30 times.

  • In MQGrader: you upload the test and the marking scheme. No marking scheme ready? You make one from the test automatically, then review and adjust it.
  • The class: you paste the list of students from the online grade book, only once.

2

Print

You

The test is printed before the lesson, with a QR code on every page. The student scans the code on any page and goes straight to where they send their paper.

  • In MQGrader: you download the PDF to print, with the QR code already on every page.

3

In class

Students

Students write the test as usual, then photograph its pages with their phone. They need no account and no app: the QR code on the test (or a link) and the phone's camera are enough. The photos reach you grouped by student, not as a heap of nameless files.

  • On the student's phone: they pick their name, take the photos, press “I sent everything” and get a ticket with a code, for the result.

4

Grading

You start it MQGrader

This is where the points are set. Every exercise is split into steps, and every step gets the points from the marking scheme. The mechanical part — reading the photos, finding the step in the marking scheme, adding up the points — can be done without you. The decision cannot.

  • In MQGrader: you press “Close uploads and start grading”. Every paper is scored step by step, with the 10 starting points.

5

Review

You

What is left are the debatable points: a solution shorter than the one in the marking scheme, an unusual notation, a dark photo, a step the student did in their head and did not write down. You check these: you accept the proposed points or change them. The decision stays yours.

  • Without the paper sheet: the answer is framed on the photo of the paper, next to the reason. It works from a phone too.

6

Results

You publish Students

At the end, each student sees their result: the points for each exercise, what they solved right and what not, and the grade. The teacher keeps the class report — which exercises went well and which did not — and the grades ready for the grade book.

  • For you: the class report, a PDF for each student and a CSV for the grade book.

Where the time goes

A test with six exercises, given to a class of 30 students, means 180 solutions to score. If one solution takes you 20 seconds — you read it, find the step in the marking scheme, write the points — you reach one hour for a single class, with no breaks. With three classes, an evening is gone.

The time is not lost on the clear cases: a right answer is seen at once, and so is a wrong one. It is lost on the debatable cases — exactly the ones you are tempted to rush, to finish.

What can be automated and what cannot

Reading the photos and proposing the points, step by step, can be automated. MQGrader uses artificial intelligence to read the paper and find, for each exercise, the steps of the marking scheme — partial points included, not only the final result.

What cannot be automated is exactly the part that matters most: the decision in a case the marking scheme does not foresee. A student who solved it differently, but correctly, must get the full points, and that is a decision for the teacher, not for a program.

The rule MQGrader is built on

The sure points grade themselves. The unsure ones wait for you. The proposed points do not reach the student until you have seen them.

Three habits that make grading easier

  1. Confirm the marking scheme before the first paper. If the marking scheme is unclear, you end up grading every paper twice.

  2. Ask for the photos in class, at the end of the lesson. A clear photo, with the whole page, is graded the first time. A dark or cut photo comes back to you, for review.

  3. Publish the results before the next test. A student who waits a week for their grade learns nothing more from it, and the talk about the old test piles up on the new one.
