QuickEval needs a new name that works in any language, a clear first market, and a look nobody else can copy.

This study compares the app with the market, picks the customers to win first, checks 16 international names, and shows MQGrader, your choice, in three design directions: logo and product screens. Your Google Doc's original dark look is drawn as a fourth, so you can compare all four on the same screens.

The short answer

Rename to MQGrader, launch in Romania, grow in Europe, use the dark and light MQ look

Name MQGrader

Your choice. MQ stands for Math & Quantitative. Students see it too, so they spread it. Keep every student screen free of AI words, and show the meaning of MQ next to the logo. Trademark and domains are not checked yet.

First customers Romanian math teachers, then private tutors

Teachers bring volume and word of mouth. Tutors pay from their own business. Then Moldova, then other European countries.

Position The teacher decides. MQGrader does the first pass.

Students upload from their phones, every paper is graded by the marking scheme, and the teacher settles each doubtful point.

Design D for dark, E for light

Your choice: deep navy, the ring-and-tick icon, Inter and mono numbers. E is the same brand in daylight. The app already switches between light and dark themes, so D and E fit that switch.

Only the brand becomes international. The plan to sell stays Romania-first: the founder's network, the test format and the lack of local competitors are all strongest there. The international name means you do not rename again when you cross the border.

Positioning statement. For math teachers who grade stacks of paper tests, MQGrader is the grading assistant that follows the marking scheme step by step. Students upload their work from their phones, every paper gets a proposed grade, and the teacher decides every doubtful point. Generic AI graders are built for US classrooms and percentages. MQGrader gives points per step, the way a marking scheme works, and it starts where that matters most: in Romania, with Subiectul I, II and III, din oficiu and grades from 1 to 10.

Where you win

  • Students upload their own work by QR. The school math graders (Examino, Frizzle) expect the teacher to photograph the stack; student upload exists mostly in university tools (Gradescope, Crowdmark).
  • The Romanian test format is native: Subiectul I, II and III, din oficiu, grades from 1 to 10.
  • The teacher stays in charge: flags, corrections, a generated marking scheme only after confirmation.
  • Print-ready test with QR, PDF reports, CSV, class analysis.
  • Built by a math teacher in Timișoara: a true story that foreign tools cannot tell.

Where you are behind

  • No published accuracy figure. Competitors claim speed and accuracy; you need real numbers.
  • Romanian interface only. The international brand needs English next.
  • No export to Google Classroom or other school tools.
  • No error types, practice sheets, or school accounts yet.
  • Grading runs on one teacher's Claude subscription. Paying customers need the API (see "Before you sell").

"Why not just use ChatGPT?"

Every teacher will ask this. The answer: a chat reads one photo at a time. MQGrader collects 28 papers by itself, grades each one by the same marking scheme, flags what it is not sure of, and gives you the class report. Names stay out of the grading answers and logs.

How to use it

Adjust before launch

  • The colors are close to Tailwind's defaults, which many sites use. Take the exact colors from the ring-and-tick icon to make them yours.
  • Keep "AI-Powered" on teacher screens only. Student screens show the brand but no AI words.
  • Grades stay 1–10 with din oficiu, not out of 100.

E · Light New

Idea: the same brand as D in daylight. White and pale-blue surfaces, the same ring-and-tick icon on a white tile, cyan-to-blue buttons, and a soft cyan glow instead of the dark halo.

Tagline: "Precision in every step." (RO: "Precizie la fiecare pas.")

Who it is for

Daytime grading, bright classrooms and projectors, and teachers who do not like dark screens. A good default for students' phones in daylight.

Watch out

Bright cyan is hard to read on white. Text and buttons use the deeper blue; the bright cyan stays in the mark and the glow.

Before you sell

Six things to settle first

  1. Move grading to the Claude API. Today the robot uses one teacher's Claude subscription token. For paying customers, use the API under commercial terms, and check Anthropic's terms. Then measure the cost of one paper and set prices from it.
  2. Prove accuracy. Grade about 200 real papers both ways: the teacher and the app. Publish how often they agree within half a point. This is the trust number every competitor lacks.
  3. GDPR for minors. Student photos are personal data of children. Write a privacy notice, a data processing agreement for schools, and a retention rule. Automatic cleanup of old files is still on the "later" list.
  4. EU AI Act. AI that evaluates learning outcomes is high-risk under Annex III. The duties apply from 2 December 2027 after the Digital Omnibus delay. Keep the teacher in charge, keep logs, document accuracy. Ask a lawyer whether "student pages never mention AI" can stay once schools deploy it.
  5. Check MQGrader. Search EUIPO TMview, the WIPO Global Brand Database and OSIM for "MQGrader" and close names in classes 9, 41 and 42. Check the .com, .app and .eu domains, and what the name means in each target language. Nothing here is verified.
  6. Separate the brands. The product gets its own name and logo; the favicon still shows the Laura Miron monogram. Keep "made by a math teacher" as the founding story, with Laura's agreement.

Next steps, in order

  1. Choose the design for MQGrader, then run the trademark and domain check.
  2. Show directions A, B and C to 5–10 math teachers and 3 tutors. Ask: "Which one would you trust with your grades?"
  3. Move grading to the API and measure the cost per paper.
  4. Run the accuracy study.
  5. Rebrand the app: the name, the logo, the favicon, and the "Your teacher uses MQGrader" line on the student pages and student PDFs. With D and E the colors and fonts change, and the existing light/dark switch carries the two themes.
  6. Add an English interface, then open the paid plans. Stripe Checkout is already in the app.
